\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{What the evidence supports.} Federated preprocessing is exact and its leakage-free protocol costs no accuracy (RQ1); under non-IID clients the decisive ingredient is a \emph{common} standardiser, which extends the StatAvg finding~\cite{bouzinis2025} to a linear pipeline and shows per-client scaling to be more damaging than per-client selection (RQ2); the price is a one-off upload and a small gap to the centralised SVM (RQ3). Exactness is an algebraic identity, so our runs check the implementation and its numerical stability rather than discover it; and because A2 is StatAvg-like, the added value over StatAvg is the audited exact selection, whose benefit we could not show.
\textbf{Consequences for the senior claims.} Federated--centralised parity holds only approximately, and was obtained with pooled preprocessing. The claim that the Pearson--LDA intersection is ``optimal''~\cite{senior2026} is not supported: on CIC-IoT2023 it trades a little accuracy for half the input width, and on N-BaIoT it can leave a linear detector unable to separate the classes. Practically: standardise with shared statistics; prefer Pearson-only selection or none unless a sufficiency check (computable from the same aggregated matrices) passes; fit every statistic on training data only; de-duplicate before splitting. The upload holds class-conditional means and covariances, which are not private; secure aggregation~\cite{bonawitz2017} or noise would be needed (not evaluated).

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conclusion}
We audited a federated linear DDoS pipeline, showed that its preprocessing is pooled and pre-split, and replaced it with FedStat-FS, an exact federated computation of variance filtering, Pearson and LDA selection and standardisation from two additive uploads per client. On two real datasets, global standardisation kept federated linear detection usable under strong non-IID skew where naive local preprocessing failed; the leakage of the original protocol was not measurable, and the hybrid intersection was neither more accurate nor stable. Future work: a federated sufficiency check for selected subsets, secure aggregation for the statistics, and multi-class typing.
